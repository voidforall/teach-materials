Reward Shaping

Win/loss is what you want; it is also nearly unlearnable from when episodes are long. Shaping bridges the gap — and, done carelessly, quietly changes the game you are playing.

Terminal reward ±1 (win/lose) is faithful: optimizing it optimizes exactly the Kaggle score. It is also sparse: in a 400-step Lux episode, the single bit at the end carries almost no gradient signal about move 7. Shaping adds dense, intermediate reward to guide learning — the question is how to do it without corrupting the objective.

Potential-based shaping: the safe road

Ng, Harada & Russell (1999) proved that shaping of the form

r' = r + γ·Φ(s') − Φ(s)

with a potential function Φ over states, provably leaves the optimal policy unchanged — the telescoping terms cancel over an episode. Φ can be a score estimate, a value-function estimate, or a heuristic "how good does this position look." Shape with potentials when you can; it converts dense guidance into a free lunch.

Heuristic shaping and its tax

Common practical shaping: reward per-step score deltas (Halite: cargo collected; Kore: fleet income), small penalties for wasted moves or suicides, bonuses for early development. These are not potential-based in general, and each one is a bet: the agent will exploit whatever pays, including in ways that hurt winning. A cargo-maximizing agent may hoard instead of cashing in; a survival-bonus agent may stall. Heuristic shaping is a crutch to be annealed away: fade it toward zero as win-rate climbs.

Multi-player credit

In zero-sum two-player games, use the symmetric frame: reward is from the learner's perspective, opponent's reward is the negative. In team or multi-episode scoring formats, check whether the competition metric is per-episode win/loss or cumulative score — shape toward the actual leaderboard metric, not a purer one you invented.

Keystone Every shaping term is a rule the agent will exploit literally.Potential-based shaping is provably safe; anything else must be small, monitored, and faded out. The terminal win/loss signal is the only term you never renegotiate.
Anti-pattern Reward hacking your own shaping.The agent discovers that avoiding terminals keeps the survival bonus flowing, or that farming a score delta beats pushing for the win. Watch episode length and shaped-vs-true reward curves together — divergence between them is the alarm bell.

Retrieve it

Write the potential-based shaping rule.

r plus gamma-phi difference.

r' = r + γΦ(s') − Φ(s). The discounted potential difference telescopes to zero over an episode, so the optimal policy is unchanged — dense guidance for free.

Why is terminal ±1 often unlearnable-from directly?

One bit, long episodes.

A single win/lose signal at step 400 gives near-zero credit to early decisions. Learning needs dense signal linking decisions to consequences within the horizon the learner can correlate.

What signals reveal reward hacking?

Shaped and true diverge.

Track shaped reward and win-rate on the same plot, plus episode length. Shaped reward rising while win-rate stalls or episodes stretch means the agent is farming your shaping, not playing your game.

Prove it to yourself

Try it — 15 minutes, on paper For Connect X, design two shapings: (a) a potential-based one with Φ = (your 4-in-a-row threats) − (opponent's); (b) a heuristic "+0.01 per own disc added". Argue which can change the optimal policy and how. Then check the Halite discussion for real shaping war stories.
