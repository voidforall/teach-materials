# Course outline — 10 lessons

1. **In the Beginning: Poe, Vidocq, and the Sensation Novel** — the 1841 birth of the genre ("The Murders in the Rue Morgue"), Dupin's ratiocination, Wilkie Collins's *The Moonstone* as the first full-length detective novel.
2. **Sherlock Holmes and the Victorian Detective** — Conan Doyle's consulting detective; the science of deduction; the short-story canon; Holmes's descendants (R. Austin Freeman's Thorndyke, Gaston Leroux).
3. **The Golden Age: The Great Puzzle (1920–1945)** — the fair-play contract; Agatha Christie, Dorothy L. Sayers, Margery Allingham, Ngaio Marsh; Knox's Ten Commandments and Van Dine's twenty rules; the Detection Club.
4. **Locked Rooms and Impossible Crimes** — John Dickson Carr and Gideon Fell; the locked-room lecture; Had-I-But-Known and the specialty of the impossible.
5. **The Hard-Boiled Revolution** — Black Mask magazine; Dashiell Hammett, Raymond Chandler, Ross Macdonald; the mean streets; style as moral instrument.
6. **The Police Procedural** — Georges Simenon's Maigret; Ed McBain's 87th Precinct; procedure, teamwork, and realism; P. D. James and Ruth Rendell as psychological bridge figures.
7. **Japan I: Honkaku and the Social School** — Edogawa Ranpo; Yokomizo Seishi's *Inugamike no Ichizoku*; shakai-ha (Matsumoto Seichō); the shin-honkaku revival (Ayatsuji Yukito's *The Decagon House Murders*).
8. **Japan II & the Wider World** — Higashino Keigo (*The Devotion of Suspect X*) as the modern mainstream; Miyabe Miyuki; the genre in translation — and why honkaku's second golden age is global now.
9. **Psychological Suspense and Noir's Daughters** — Patricia Highsmith; Ira Levin; Gillian Flynn and domestic noir; Tana French's literary police novels; Nordic noir (Sjöwall & Wahlöö → Larsson, Nesbø); the serial-killer thriller (Thomas Harris).
10. **Capstone: Reading Like a Detective** — the fair-play checklist; building your own reading map by school; active-reading habits (clue tracking, narrator reliability); a spoiler-free personal canon.
