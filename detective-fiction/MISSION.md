# Mission: Detective Fiction — A Reader's Map of the Genre

## Why
The user loves detective/mystery fiction and wants a systematic map of the genre: how it developed from Poe to the present, the major schools (classic puzzle / Golden Age, hard-boiled, police procedural, Japanese honkaku & social school, psychological suspense, Nordic noir), and the representative authors and works of each — as a basis for building a discriminating reading list. Strictly **no spoilers**: no plot resolutions, culprits, or twists are ever revealed.

## Success looks like
- Sketch the genre's timeline: Poe → Collins/Doyle → Golden Age → hard-boiled → procedural → psychological/Nordic/Japanese schools → today.
- Name 2–3 representative authors and works per school, and say what distinguishes each school's detective, crime, style, and "contract with the reader".
- Explain fair play (Knox / Van Dine rules) and know how to judge whether a mystery plays fair.
- Pick the right next book for a given mood using the school taxonomy.
- Read a mystery actively: tracking clues, red herrings, and narrative voice.

## Constraints
- **No spoilers, ever** — describe setups and themes only; never reveal culprits or twists.
- Bilingual: every lesson ships as `NNNN-slug.html` + `NNNN-slug.zh.html`.
- Audience: an avid general reader, not an academic — lively, concrete, citation-backed.
- Short lessons with a retrieval quiz and a small reading/observation task.

## Out of scope
- Academic literary theory beyond what helps a reader choose books.
- Screen adaptations except as signposts to the books.
