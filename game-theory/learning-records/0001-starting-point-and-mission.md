# Starting point: CS learner, "some exposure," strategy + markets mission

Established at course start (2026-07-12). The learner has a **CS background** and **some
exposure** to game theory — knows the Prisoner's Dilemma and the *term* Nash equilibrium, but is
shaky on actually *solving* games and on the formalism. Wants **balanced** math (intuition +
core notation, not proof-heavy). Mission is game theory as a **working tool** for two linked ends:
**strategic decisions** and **trading/markets** (auctions, microstructure, adversarial multi-agent).
See [[MISSION.md]].

**Implications for future sessions:**
- Teach the *solving toolkit* as executable skill, not vocabulary. Lesson 1 delivered the
  best-response "underline method" for pure Nash equilibria — the load-bearing procedure.
- Use famous games as familiar anchors, don't re-derive them; ground new material in
  pricing/entry/auction/competition examples per the mission and [[NOTES.md]].
- The CS/mechanism-design/computation angle is an asset — lean into it later (algorithmic
  game theory, price of anarchy, auctions).
- Zone of proximal development after Lesson 1: **mixed strategies** (the lesson-1 quiz already
  flags "zero pure equilibria → randomize"), then sequential games / backward induction
  (commitment & credibility are trading-relevant), then market games (Cournot/Bertrand) and
  auctions/mechanism design.

**Not yet demonstrated:** Lesson 1 is *covered*, not yet *retrieved under test*. Watch for
evidence — a correct, unaided pure-NE call on an unfamiliar matrix (the "you drive" task) —
before treating the underline method as learned.
