# Mission: Game Theory — Strategic Reasoning for Decisions & Markets

## Why
I want game theory as a **working tool for thinking**, not a spectator subject. Two linked payoffs:
1. **Strategic decisions** — negotiation, competition, pricing, entry/exit, and everyday
   multi-party choices where my best move depends on what others do.
2. **Trading & markets** — auctions, market microstructure, and adversarial/multi-agent
   settings that connect to my orderbook and trading-agent work.

I come from a **CS background**, so I like precise models and mechanism; I just haven't
drilled the game-theory formalism.

## Success looks like
- Given any small strategic situation, I can **model it as a game** (players, actions, payoffs)
  and **solve it** — find the equilibria and say what rational players actually do.
- I fluently use the core toolkit: dominance, best response, **pure & mixed Nash equilibrium**,
  backward induction, and subgame-perfect equilibrium.
- I can reason about **markets as games**: Bertrand/Cournot competition, first- and
  second-price auctions, and why a second-price auction makes truth-telling optimal.
- I recognize the CS bridge: Nash equilibria as fixed points, price of anarchy, and the basics
  of **mechanism design** (designing the game so good behavior is self-interested).
- I can spot the classic strategic structures in the wild (commitment, credibility, signaling,
  the winner's curse) and name the move.

## Starting point
- **Some exposure**: I know the famous games (Prisoner's Dilemma) and the term "Nash
  equilibrium," but I'm shaky on *solving* games and on the formalism.
- Want **balanced** math: intuition first, then the core notation worked by hand — not
  proof-heavy, not hand-wavy.

## Constraints
- Short, self-contained lessons I can finish in a sitting.
- Ground examples in **strategy and markets**, not abstract puzzles, wherever possible.
- Beautiful, re-readable reference docs (I'll come back to them).

## Out of scope (for now)
- Heavy proof-theoretic treatment (existence proofs, fixed-point topology) — later, if wanted.
- Cooperative game theory (Shapley value, coalitions) — deprioritized behind non-cooperative.
- Building a full trading agent from this — the theory first, application as it becomes useful.
