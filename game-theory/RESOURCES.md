# Game Theory Resources

## Knowledge

- [Course: Open Yale ECON 159 — Game Theory (Ben Polak)](https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-159)
  Free, complete video course with transcripts and problem sets. The gold-standard intuition-first
  introduction: dominance, best response, Nash equilibrium, backward induction, signaling. Use for:
  the primary "watch this" source for nearly every early lesson. *(Grounds Lesson 1.)*
- [Book: _An Introduction to Game Theory_ — Martin J. Osborne (author's site)](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/osborne/igt/)
  Clean undergraduate text; draft chapters and [publicly-available solutions](https://www.economics.utoronto.ca/osborne/igt/solsp5.pdf)
  on the author's own site. Balanced formalism — matches the "balanced math" preference. Use for:
  precise definitions, best-response functions, worked exercises to drill.
- [Book: _Algorithmic Game Theory_ — Nisan, Roughgarden, Tardos, Vazirani (free PDF, CMU)](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~sandholm/cs15-892F13/algorithmic-game-theory.pdf)
  The CS bridge: Nash as computation, auctions, mechanism design, price of anarchy. Use for: the
  trading/markets + CS-background arc, once the fundamentals are solid. *(Verify chapter links per use.)*
- [Reference: SEP — Game Theory (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/game-theory/)
  Rigorous, well-sourced conceptual reference. Use for: pinning down a definition or a subtle
  distinction (e.g. weak vs strict dominance, refinements) when a lesson needs precision.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/GAMETHEORY](https://www.reddit.com/r/GAMETHEORY/)
  Focused subreddit for problems, intuition checks, and "is my equilibrium analysis right?" threads.
  Use for: sanity-checking a solved game and asking why a model behaves oddly.
- [Economics Stack Exchange — game-theory tag](https://economics.stackexchange.com/questions/tagged/game-theory)
  High-signal Q&A with worked, citable answers. Use for: precise technical questions once you can
