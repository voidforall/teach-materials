Failing a Message, Properly

Dead-letter exchanges, the retry ladder with backoff, and the parking lot for poison.

Lesson 2 gave you nack, and a warning I deferred: nack(requeue=true) sends the message straight back to the head of the queue — instant redelivery, hot loop, same failure, forever. This lesson is the production answer: a mechanical pipeline for messages that can't be handled right now.1

Dead-lettering: the redirect you configure, not code

A queue can declare a dead-letter exchange (DLX) — "where messages go when they leave this queue without being consumed." Three triggers:2

What dead-letters a message out of a queue
TriggerWhen
rejectedconsumer nack/reject with requeue=false
expiredmessage TTL ran out (per-message or per-queue)
overflowqueue hit its max length — oldest messages evicted

The dead-lettered message is re-published to the DLX with all its original properties plus an x-death header (queue it came from, reason, count, timestamps). From there, normal routing applies — which is the trick everything below exploits: the DLX can route back into your own topology, not just into an error bucket.

Keystone A queue's TTL expiry dead-letters the message; the DLX routes it back to the work queue. Put a queue in front of that redirect and you've built a timer — a delayed retry — with zero code. Backoff is just a ladder of such queues with growing TTLs. The broker does the sleeping; your consumer only ever sees "try again, later."

The retry ladder

Failure path: q.orders dead-letters into the retry exchange → lands in a TTL queue → expiry routes it back to the work queue. After too many deaths (x-death count), route to a parking lot instead of around again.

Walk one message through: the consumer fails on it → nack(requeue=false) → q.orders dead-letters it to the retry exchange → it sits in retry.10s for ten seconds → TTL expiry dead-letters it back to work → the consumer tries again. Third failure: the retry exchange sees x-death.count ≥ 3 and routes it to parking — a plain queue with no consumer, whose job is to be watched by humans and dashboards.

Two traps, both common (1) In-memory sleep in the handler — blocks a consumer slot (prefetch, Lesson 2) while doing nothing; under load your whole worker pool is sleeping. Never sleep in the handler; let the broker hold the message. (2) Per-message TTL in a shared retry queue — expiry is only checked at the queue's head, so one long-TTL message pins shorter ones behind it (head-of-line blocking). One TTL per queue — that's why it's a ladder of queues.

Poison messages, and cycle protection

A poison message fails every time — malformed payload, missing reference, bug in the handler. It can never succeed, so retrying is pure waste; worse, it clogs the queue and starves good messages. The ladder handles it automatically via the x-death count: capped retries, then parking lot. The parking lot is not a bug tracker — wire it to one (alert, dump to a table, ticket) or nobody will ever look.

RabbitMQ also protects against immediate cycles: a message dead-lettered back to a queue it already passed through, with nothing changed, is dropped rather than spun forever. Your ladder avoids cycles anyway — every hop changes TTL queue and bumps the count.

Check yourself

Q1. Why a ladder of TTL queues instead of one retry queue?

Expiry is only checked at a queue's head.

Mixed TTLs in one queue head-of-line block: one 5-minute message pins every 10-second message behind it. One TTL per queue avoids it.

Q2. Which nack call puts a message on the retry path?

nack with requeue set to false.

requeue=true is the instant-redelivery hot loop from Lesson 2; false triggers the queue's dead-lettering.

Q3. What distinguishes a retryable failure from poison, mechanically?

The x-death count after repeated failures.

You can't know the future — so you cap attempts (count ≥ 3) and park. Transient errors recover within the ladder; poison never does.

Q4. Who "sleeps" during a five-minute backoff?

Nobody — the message waits in the broker's retry queue.

The consumer keeps processing other messages; handler-side sleeps would burn prefetch slots and stall the pool.

Now you drive

Design it — before you reveal

Your email-sending consumer fails whenever the SMTP provider 500s (transient, recovers in ~a minute) and also occasionally gets an address like nobody@invalid (permanent). Design the failure path: exchanges, queues, TTLs, bindings, and what the consumer does differently in each failure case. Hint: can the consumer tell the two cases apart before it nacks?

Reveal one good answer

Yes — classify before nack. A 5xx from the provider is retryable: nack(requeue=false) onto the ladder (10s → 1m → 5m, then park). A rejected address (4xx / validation failure) is poison — retrying can never fix it, so don't even start the ladder: either publish straight to parking, or keep a two-hop micro-ladder (one retry) for safety. Beyond that, the topology is exactly the ladder diagram: q.emails with DLX → retry exchange → TTL queues bound by routing key; TTL expiry routes back to work; binding x-death ≥ 3 → parking.

The deep point: retry policy is per-failure-class, and the consumer is the only place that knows the class. The ladder handles "later might help"; parking handles "later never helps"; classification is a code responsibility the topology can't do for you.
