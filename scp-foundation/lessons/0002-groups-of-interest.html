SCP Foundation · Reading the Lore Lesson 02

Groups of Interest

The Foundation isn't alone. Six other ways to want an anomaly — and why that texture makes the world feel real.

Lesson 1 gave you the reading lens for a single article. This one zooms out to the world. Because here's the thing casual readers miss: the Foundation is just one organisation with one philosophy — "lock it away, keep the world normal." Plenty of others in the setting want the exact same anomalies for completely different reasons.

The wiki calls them Groups of Interest (GoIs): "any organisation involved in the paranormal world"1 — corporations, cults, governments, art collectives. When one shows up in an article, it carries a whole worldview with it. Recognising them is what turns a standalone file into a scene in a much bigger story.

Keystone idea Don't memorise GoIs as a list. File each one by what it wants an anomaly for, and its stance toward the Foundation falls out automatically. Motive → stance.

The filing system: six motives

1 · The mirror enemy — Chaos Insurgency

CHAOS INSURGENCY
Former Foundation personnel who defected and now want to tear it down and rip open the Veil — through sabotage and terror. They use anomalies as weapons.1
Wants: power. Stance: the Foundation's dark reflection and most personal enemy.

Why it lands: they were us. Same training, same access, opposite ethics. The most intimate kind of villain.

2 · The rival cop — Global Occult Coalition (GOC)

GLOBAL OCCULT COALITION
A UN-affiliated paranormal law enforcement body. Same goal as the Foundation — protect normal people — but their answer is to destroy anomalies, not contain them.1
Wants: a clean world. Stance: uneasy ally / rival — friction over "contain vs. kill."

Why it lands: not evil, just a different doctrine. The best conflicts in SCP are two "good guys" who can't agree on method.

3 · The believers — a flesh-vs-metal holy war

Two religions that mirror each other perfectly. Keep them as a pair and you'll never confuse them:

CHURCH OF THE BROKEN GOD
Metal. A mechanistic faith: flesh is a flaw, and their shattered god must be reassembled from anomalous machine-parts. They hunt Foundation anomalies as holy relics.1
Wants: divine machinery. Stance: adversary — you're sitting on their god's body.
SARKIC CULTS (Nälkä)
Flesh. Secret cults of ritual cannibalism and organic manipulation — reshaping living tissue as worship. Potential extinction-level (XK) threats.1
Wants: mastery of flesh. Stance: adversary — and genuinely horrifying.

Machine vs. meat, salvation-through-perfection vs. salvation-through-flesh. That symmetry is deliberate and it's some of the deepest lore on the wiki.

4 · The merchants — Marshall, Carter & Dark Ltd.

MARSHALL, CARTER & DARK LTD. ("MC&D")
An elite broker of anomalous goods for the ultra-rich — an anomaly auction house. Their deals trigger containment breaches and leak classified info.1
Wants: profit. Stance: amoral rival — anomalies are inventory, not threats.

Why it lands: pure capitalism applied to the impossible. Chilling precisely because it's so mundane a motive.

5 · The artists — Are We Cool Yet? (AWCY?)

ARE WE COOL YET?
Loose collectives of "anartists" who make anomalous art — often fatal public installations. Exposure is the point, which makes them a walking threat to the secrecy of the whole setting.1
Wants: to be seen. Stance: chaotic, unpredictable — anti-Veil by nature.

6 · The liberators — The Serpent's Hand

THE SERPENT'S HAND
Small but formidable — they believe anomalies (especially sentient ones) deserve freedom, not cages. They operate from the anomalous Wanderers' Library.1
Wants: liberation. Stance: opposes containment on principle — the moral counter-argument to the Foundation itself.

Why it lands: they force the uncomfortable question the Foundation avoids — is locking a thinking being in a box right?

The payoff

Next time an article mentions a rival group, you won't just read a name — you'll feel the collision of worldviews behind it. A breach caused by MC&D reads differently from one caused by the Chaos Insurgency. That's the texture that makes 8,000 files feel like one living world.

Try it — match motive to group

Recall first, then open. Aim for the motive, not just the name.

1. Ex-Foundation defectors who want it destroyed?

The Chaos Insurgency — the mirror enemy.

Same origin, opposite ethics. Anomalies as weapons.

2. Wants the same safety, but kills anomalies instead of caging them?

The Global Occult Coalition — the rival cop.

Shared goal, opposite method. "Contain vs. destroy."

3. Sells anomalies to billionaires for profit?

Marshall, Carter and Dark — the merchants.

Amoral broker. Anomalies are luxury inventory.

4. The metal religion rebuilding a machine god?

The Church of the Broken God — the believers.

Flesh is flawed; god must be reassembled. Pairs with Sarkic (flesh).

5. Wants to free sentient anomalies from containment?

The Serpent's Hand — the liberators there.

The moral counter-argument to the Foundation itself.
