Navigating the Archive

10,000+ articles. Most are ordinary. Here's how to walk straight to the great ones.

You now have the class lens, the cast, and the reading checklist. The last barrier between you and a lifetime of great reading is volume. The wiki has over ten thousand SCP articles, and — like any open collaborative site — most are unremarkable and some are rough. Appreciation isn't about reading everything. It's about finding the gold.

Keystone idea — the mistake almost everyone makes SCP numbers are chronological, not ranked. Low numbers are the oldest articles, written when the site was young and rougher. Do not start at SCP-001 and read upward — you'll hit the weakest era first. The guide is explicit: newer content is generally superior; start from the top-rated lists instead.1

This one fact fixes the most common beginner mistake. The famous early ones (173, 096, 682) are famous despite their era, not because low numbers are better. Behind them sit thousands of newer, more polished works you'd never reach by counting upward.

Three kinds of thing to read

The universe is bigger than the anomaly files. Three content types:1

SCP ARTICLES
The clinical anomaly files you know — the SCP-#### documents. The backbone.
TALES
Ordinary fiction set in the Foundation universe — narrative stories, no clinical format. Characters, plots, emotion. Where the world becomes human. Start: Tales Hub.
CANONS
Curated shared universes — "multiple authors building on each others' work" with internal consistency. The deep-lore reading, once a theme grabs you. Start: Canon Hub.

If a single SCP article ever makes you want "more of this world," the answer is almost always a Tale or a Canon.

The gold-finding toolkit

  • Top Rated Pages — community-upvoted best-of. Available all-time, this month, and by year.1 Your default front door. All-time = the canon of greatness; by-year = watch the craft evolve.
  • Curated Lists — reader-made collections organised by genre and theme (horror, humour, tragedy, mind-bending).1 Best when you're in a specific mood.
  • Tag search — filter by topic/property (e.g. keter, humanoid, temporal). Precision tool once you know what flavour you like.
  • Follow the links. Great articles hyperlink to related ones. Wandering outward from one you loved is often the best path.1
  • Ask the community. r/SCP and the official forums give personalised recs far better than any algorithm.1

One trap to know: SCP-001

You'd expect SCP-001 to be "the first / most important." It's the opposite — a special reserved slot holding multiple competing "proposals," several different authors' takes on what the Foundation's first anomaly might be. They're deliberately mysterious and lean on lore you won't have yet. Don't start with 001; come back once you're steeped in the world.1

A simple starting route

  1. Open Top Rated (all-time). Read three that grab you by title.
  2. Apply the Lesson 3 checklist as you read — feel the machine work.
  3. Loved one? Follow its links, or find its author's other work.
  4. Wanted "more world"? Jump to a Tale or Canon.
  5. Stuck? Ask r/SCP for a rec in your favourite flavour.

Notice the range: infinite furniture, a tickle blob, a picture-only file, a memory hole, a lore epic. That spread — comedy to cosmic dread in the same clinical voice — is the whole appeal. Read across it, not just the scary end.

Try it — recall, don't peek

1. Do low SCP numbers mean better or more important articles?

No — numbers are chronological, so oldest.

Low = earliest, rougher era. Start from top-rated instead.

2. What's a Tale, versus an SCP article?

Narrative fiction, not a clinical anomaly file.

Characters and plot in the universe; no containment format.

3. What is a Canon on the wiki?

A curated shared universe multiple authors build.

Internally consistent deep lore, unlike the no-canon default.

4. Should a newcomer start by reading SCP-001?

No — it's cryptic proposals needing prior lore.

A reserved slot of competing takes. Come back later.
