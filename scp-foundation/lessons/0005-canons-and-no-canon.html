Canons & "There Is No Canon"

The paradox at the heart of the whole project — and how resolving it sets you free to read.

Back a couple of lessons you asked the right question: do the other organisations exist across the whole world, or just in individual stories? The answer was "both — because there is no canon." This finale unpacks that slogan properly, because understanding it is the difference between feeling lost in SCP and feeling at home in it.

The slogan that confuses everyone

New readers hear "there is no canon" and assume it means nothing connects — every article floats alone. That's wrong, and the wiki itself pushes back:

Straight from the Canon Hub "The idea that there is no canon is a bit silly at times. It's not that we don't have any — it's that we have a multitude which touch, cross, and dip into each other."1

So it's not zero canon. It's many canons, overlapping — and none of them ranked above the others as "the true one."

What a canon actually is

A canon is an opt-in shared universe: a storyline multiple authors agree to build inside, with internal consistency, "building on each others' work."1 Inside a canon, events and continuity do matter. Between canons — and out in the wider wiki — they don't have to.

Think of it as layers:

THE WHOLE WIKI
No enforced canon. Any article may contradict any other. Maximum freedom.
A NAMED CANON
A curated pocket universe with its own consistent continuity, that authors deliberately write into.
A SINGLE ARTICLE / SERIES
One author's take. May belong to a canon, or stand entirely alone.
Keystone idea — this sets you free There is no "correct version" to memorise. When two articles contradict, neither is wrong — they're different authors, possibly different canons. Stop asking "which is real?" and start asking "what is this author choosing to do with the setting?" That reframe is the whole secret to reading SCP without anxiety.

A sampler of named canons

Each is a doorway into a consistent, deeper world. Pick by theme, not by number:1

Broken Masquerade
The Foundation's secrecy fails — anomalies become public knowledge. "What if the world found out?"
End of Death
Death itself stops working. Immortality as a slow catastrophe.
Third Law
Street-level world where magic and technology converge. Grounded, urban supernatural.
Those Twisted Pines
Site-64 in the Pacific Northwest — including the anomalous city Three Portlands.
Resurrection
A reconstruction of the Foundation's central storyline — a "main quest" for the setting.
NIGHTFALL / Daybreak
End-of-world canons: eternal night after a goddess's death; survival after a solar catastrophe.

…and many more on the Canon Hub. Each has a hub page with a suggested reading order — that's how you read a canon without getting lost.

Where wisdom lives — go read with people

Everything so far is knowledge and skill. The last ingredient — wisdom — only comes from reading alongside others who argue about it. Two high-signal places:

  • r/SCP — active, welcoming; great for "what should I read next in this mood" and "help me understand this ending."
  • The official SCP Wiki forums — where authors and long-time readers discuss intent and canon. Best for deep-lore questions.

No pressure to post. But lurking a recommendation thread will teach you what the community treasures faster than any list — mine included.

Try it — recall, don't peek

1. Does "there is no canon" mean articles never connect?

No — it means many overlapping canons exist.

Not zero, but a multitude that touch and cross.

2. Two great articles contradict each other. Which one is wrong?

Neither — different authors, possibly different canons.

There's no single true version to be wrong against.

3. What is a named canon, precisely?

An opt-in shared universe with internal consistency.

Continuity matters inside it; not across the wider wiki.

4. Best question to ask when two versions clash?

"What is this author doing with the setting?"

Intent over truth. That reframe removes the anxiety.
