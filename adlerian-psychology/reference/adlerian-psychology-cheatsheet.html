Adlerian Psychology — Ideas and a Way of Living

The whole ten-lesson course on one page: what Adler claimed, and how to live by it.

1 · Key-idea map

IdeaIn one lineLesson
Teleology vs etiologyWe are moved by goals in the present, not caused by wounds in the past; symptoms serve a purpose[1]1
Inferiority feeling (Minderwertigkeitsgefühl)Universal and useful — the engine of growth; it becomes a wound only when used as an excuse (inferiority complex)2
Superiority complexThe counterfeit of striving: acting superior to avoid, not to grow2
Lifestyle (Lebensstil)Your personal grammar of goal, self-image, and worldview — formed early, but revisable; the creative self authors it3
Social interest (Gemeinschaftsgefühl)Community feeling is the compass of mental health; cooperation over competition[2]4
Three life tasks (Lebensaufgaben)Work, friendship, love — each a relation of cooperation; each avoided only at the cost of courage5
Separation of tasksAsk: whose task is this? You cannot make others change, and they cannot live your life for you6
Horizontal vs vertical relationsEqual-but-not-the-same beats ranks; praise and rebuke both speak from above7
Encouragement vs praisePraise judges from above ("well done"); encouragement stands beside ("thank you, that helped")7
Self-acceptance vs self-affirmationAcceptance sees the irreplaceable this-self, flaws included; affirmation lies to the self about them8
Courage to be ordinaryBeing normal is not failure; the guiding star matters more than perfection8
Happiness = feeling of contributionNot recognition received, but contribution felt — the Adlerian definition of happiness9
Dynamism: life as danceLife is not a line to a summit; each moment can be complete, like a dance[3]9

2 · Decision flowchart: whose task is it?

Keystone Facing any problem, ask one question first: whose task is this — who ultimately bears the consequences? 
Any problem
  │
  ├─ Whose task is it? (who bears the consequences?)
  │
  ├─ MINE ──────────► Act with courage; do it even without applause;
  │                    accept that others may judge — that is their task.
  │
  └─ THEIRS ────────► Do not take it over; do not issue verdicts.
                       Assist only if asked; help, don't steer.
                       "Not living others' lives."

Most anger and most anxiety live in someone else's task. Hand it back.

3 · Adler vs Freud

QuestionFreudAdler
What moves us?Causes — etiology: early experience and trauma determine the personGoals — teleology: we choose meanings that serve present aims
Which time matters?The past, especially childhoodThe present and the future one is moving toward
Core driveBiological drives (sexuality, aggression)Social striving: from felt inferiority toward significance and belonging
What is a symptom?Expression of inner conflictA tool serving a (often unconscious) goal
Therapist's stanceExpert who interprets the patient's hidden material, from aboveEncourager in a horizontal relation who helps the client revise their lifestyle

5 · Life attitude charter

  1. My feelings serve my goals; I can choose the goals.
  2. No experience decides my life — only the meaning I give it.
  3. Inferiority is an engine, not an excuse.
  4. I write my own lifestyle, and I can revise it.
  5. My worth is proven in contribution, not in applause.
  6. I do my task; I let others do theirs — without verdicts.
  7. I meet everyone horizontally: encouragement and gratitude, never praise-and-rebuke.
  8. I accept this irreplaceable self, and I have the courage to be ordinary.
  9. Happiness is the feeling of contribution, here and now.
  10. Life is a dance, not a climb — each moment can be complete.

6 · Seven-day practice plan

DayPracticeTrains
1Catch one "because of my past…" story; restate it as "so that I might…"Teleology
2Notice one inferiority feeling today; name what it pushes you to learn or doInferiority as engine
3Sort today's worries into "my task / their task"; act on one of yoursSeparation of tasks
4Replace one piece of praise (given or craved) with encouragement or thanksHorizontal relations
5Do one thing for someone with no possibility of recognition; note the feelingContribution
6Log each comparison you catch with others; rephrase it horizontallyCourage to be ordinary
7Spend the day treating each moment as complete in itself — dance, don't climbDynamism

7 · Reading list

  • Adler, Understanding Human Nature (1927) — the most readable Adler by Adler.
  • Adler, What Life Should Mean to You (1931) — lifestyle, the life tasks, and the life attitude, in his own voice.
  • Adler, The Practice and Theory of Individual Psychology — the essays where the system is built.
  • Dreikurs, Social Equality: The Challenge of Today and Children: The Challenge — Adlerian democracy applied to society and to parenting.
  • Kishimi & Koga, The Courage to Be Disliked (2013; EN 2018) — the Socratic dialogue behind this course's life-attitude lessons.
