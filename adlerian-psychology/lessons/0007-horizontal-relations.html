Horizontal and Vertical Relationships

Adler's most counterintuitive instruction: stop praising people. Praise, like rebuke, is an act performed from above — and life keeps its hierarchy as long as any relation stays vertical.

Two geometries are possible between people. A vertical relationship places one above the other — judge and judged, evaluator and evaluated. A horizontal relationship places them side by side — different in ability, role, and age, but equal in personhood, comrades in the same here and now. The Courage to Be Disliked makes the strong claim: you cannot mix the two. If even one of your relationships is vertical, your life as a whole stays organized around rank — because the mind keeps one grammar, not two. The choice of geometry is the lifestyle's deepest layer, deeper than which particular people are in your life.

Why praise is vertical

Praise feels kind, which is why this stings. But look at its grammar: "well done" is a verdict, and verdicts are issued from above — it is what a superior says to a subordinate. Praise that targets ability ("you're so smart", "good girl") embeds a hierarchy: someone with the standing to certify someone else's worth. It also does measurable damage — the psychology of praise since Dweck shows ability-praise makes learners fear exactly the challenges that would grow them, because failure now threatens the label. The praised become performers of verdicts.

Rebuke, obviously, is also vertical — but for a subtler reason than cruelty: anger is cheap. Shouting requires no respect, no knowledge of the other person, no sincerity. It is the tool of the person who has given up on equality.

What to do instead: encouragement

The horizontal alternatives, per Adler and his student Dreikurs (whose work with children defined the field), are encouragement, gratitude, and respect:

  • Gratitude over praise: "Your notes saved me an hour" instead of "great job on the notes." Gratitude reports my benefit; it judges no one — contribution-language, not verdict-language (Lesson 4).
  • Concrete thanks over generic praise: say what happened and to whom it helped.
  • Sincere opinion over rebuke: disagree plainly — "I see it differently, and here's why" — as an equal among equals, without the verdict's thunder.

Dreikurs's parenting rule compresses it: neither punish nor praise; encourage. The child (or colleague, or partner) who is thanked for contribution learns that they matter to the community; the one who is praised learns that they are being ranked — and either craves the ranking or dreads it.

The self-audit

Apply the geometry to yourself: when you help someone, do you resent their ingratitude? Then you were, in your own accounting, purchasing a verdict. When someone outperforms you, do you feel diminished? That is vertical arithmetic — their plus as your minus, the competitive suffering of Lesson 4. Horizontal life is not sameness of ability; it is refusing to let difference become rank.

KeystoneLife stays vertical while any relationship is. Praise and rebuke are both verdicts from above; encouragement, gratitude, and respect are what equality sounds like in daily speech. Choose the geometry — the words follow.
Anti-patternThe praise economy. Homes, schools, and workplaces run on verdicts — stars, rankings, "good job," "disappointing" — training people to work for evaluation instead of contribution. Its graduates (we) then need applause to feel real, and read every interaction as a score. Adler offers the exit ramp: speak benefit, not judgment.

Retrieve it

What makes praise a vertical act?

It is a verdict issued from above

"Well done" certifies another's worth from a superior position; it embeds hierarchy and trains the receiver to perform for verdicts.

What are the horizontal replacements for praise and rebuke?

Encouragement, gratitude, and respect

Gratitude reports my benefit without judging; sincere disagreement speaks as an equal — contribution-language instead of verdict-language.

Why can't you mix vertical and horizontal relations?

The mind keeps one grammar

If one relationship stays vertical, rank organizes the whole lifestyle; equality is all-or-nothing in grammar, not in people count.

Prove it to yourself

Try it — this week Ban verdict-speech for seven days: no "good job," no "you should have," no sarcasm-as-correction. Replace each with a benefit-statement ("that helped me because ___") or a sincere equal-view ("I'd do it differently, here's why"). Watch two things: how often you were verdicting (the count will humiliate you — respond with self-acceptance, Lesson 8), and how conversations change when the scoreboard is off.
