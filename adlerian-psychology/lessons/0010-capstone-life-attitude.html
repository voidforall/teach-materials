Capstone: The Adlerian Life Attitude

No new theory — the assembly. Nine lessons become one charter of attitude and values, and a seven-day plan for living it.

The mission asked two questions: what are Adler's main ideas, and what attitude toward life do they prescribe? You now hold the ideas. This lesson compresses them into a charter you can keep — then does the one thing a charter needs: schedules practice.

The charter

From ideas to attitudes — the whole course in nine shifts
LessonIdeaAttitude shift
1TeleologyMy feelings serve my goals; I ask "what is this for?" — and I can choose new goals.
2Inferiority feelingsI hear "not yet," never "not good enough"; inferiority is fuel, not a verdict.
3Lifestyle"That's just who I am" is a draft I can revise; I am the author, not the transcript.
4Social interestMy worth is contribution; I belong where I give, and I refuse the ranking of persons.
5Life tasksWork, friendship, love: I close the distance with courage — advancing while afraid.
6Task separationI do my task fully and release everyone else's; I live my task, not my audience.
7Horizontal relationsI speak benefit, not verdicts — no praise, no rebuke, encouragement instead.
8Self-acceptanceWorth is not a wage; I dare to be ordinary and drop the exceptionalism wager.
9DynamismLife is a dance, happiness is the feeling of contributing now — no certificate needed.

Read as one sentence: I am a goal-directed author among equals, whose worth is contribution, whose tasks are mine alone, whose courage advances despite fear, and whose life happens now. That is the Adlerian attitude.

DayPracticeTrains

Adler's long echo

Worth knowing as you leave: Adler's ideas outlived the movement's name. Dreikurs carried them into parenting and classrooms; his emphasis on goals and the social embeddedness of disturbance echoes through cognitive-behavioral therapy; his unconditional regard and growth-orientation anticipate humanistic psychology (Maslow and Rogers) and much of positive psychology. The ideas you have just absorbed are, in many ways, the common sense of modern psychology — it took a century to catch up.

KeystoneThe course in three words: goals, contribution, now. Understand yourself teleologically, value yourself by contribution, and live it in the present moment — with courage, across clean boundaries, among equals.
Anti-patternWeaponized Adler.Turning the course into a new stick to beat yourself with — "I should be courageous, horizontal, contributing, present, starting immediately." That voice is a verdict issued from above, and the one above is you: vertical grammar (L7), recognition-seeking from your own inner judge (L6), worth treated as a wage again (L8). Apply Adler gently — especially to yourself. Change is a task you approach with courage, not a standard you fail.
