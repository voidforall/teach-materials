# Mission: Valuation Basics — estimating what an asset is worth

## Why
The user wants to stop guessing what companies (and other cash-generating assets) are worth. Following Aswath Damodaran's approach: valuation is a bridge between story and numbers. The goal is to build, defend, and critique a valuation — not to memorize formulas.

## Success looks like
- Distinguish **price** (what the market pays) from **value** (what the asset is worth) and know which one a given analysis is actually producing.
- Build a simple discounted cash flow (DCF) valuation: forecast cash flows, pick discount rates, estimate growth and terminal value, arrive at an intrinsic value per share.
- Decompose any multiple (P/E, EV/EBITDA) into its drivers and spot when comparables are not comparable.
- Recognize the standard biases (confirmation, anchoring, certainty) that corrupt valuations — in others' work and one's own.
- Read a Damodaran lecture/blog post and reproduce his arithmetic.

## Constraints
- Grounded in Aswath Damodaran's NYU course, books (*Investment Valuation*, *The Little Book of Valuation*, *Damodaran on Valuation*), datasets and blog ("Musings on Markets").
- Lessons short and mechanism-first: show the equation, then the intuition, then the practice.
- Bilingual: every lesson ships in English and Chinese.

## Out of scope
- Option pricing / real options beyond awareness (Lesson 1 mentions it only).
- Advanced topics: financial companies, private-company illiquidity adjustments in depth, sum-of-the-parts — later courses.
