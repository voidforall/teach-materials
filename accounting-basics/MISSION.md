# Mission: Accounting Basics — reading company statements as an investor

## Why
The user wants to understand company earnings and financial statements so they can evaluate businesses they invest in — not to become an accountant. The goal is practical statement literacy: pick up a 10-K or earnings release and know what the numbers say, what they hide, and what questions to ask next. Companion to the `valuation-basics` course: accounting supplies the inputs that valuation consumes.

## Success looks like
- Explain what the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement each report, and how the three articulate (net income → retained earnings; cash flow reconciles income to cash).
- Go from revenue to net income to EPS on a real income statement, and know which line items are "real" vs accounting artifacts.
- Read a balance sheet through the investor's lens: what the company owns, owes, and whether equity is growing for the right reason.
- Distinguish earnings from cash, spot the accruals gap, and judge earnings quality.
- Compute and interpret the core investor ratios (margins, ROE, DuPont decomposition, current ratio, debt/equity) — and know each one's blind spot.
- Recognize the standard red flags of earnings manipulation (revenue games, expense games, non-GAAP padding) before they become headlines.
- Sit down with a real 10-K/annual report and extract the essentials in under an hour.

## Constraints
- Investor-first: every concept must earn its place by changing a real investing decision. Bookkeeping mechanics (debits/credits, journal entries) stay out.
- Bilingual: every lesson ships in English and Chinese.
- Lessons short and mechanism-first: show the statement/equation, then the intuition, then the practice.

## Out of scope
- Becoming a bookkeeper (journal entries, trial balances).
- Tax accounting, consolidation/IFRS-vs-GAAP minutiae, financial-company statements.
- Full valuation models — that's the `valuation-basics` course next door.
