Accounting Basics — One-Page Cheat Sheet

Every tool from the course, on one printable page. Print it, keep it next to the 10-K.

The three statements

  • Income statement — did we make a profit? (a period)
  • Cash flow statement — where did the cash go? (the same period, in cash)
  • Balance sheet — what do we own and owe? (one day's snapshot: assets = liabilities + equity)

Articulation: net income flows into retained earnings (minus dividends); the cash flow statement ends exactly at the balance sheet's cash line. Lies must be papered over at the seams.

Income statement ladder

Revenue → − COGS → Gross profit → − OpEx → EBIT → − Interest → − Tax → Net income → ÷ diluted shares → EPS

Balance sheet highlights

  • Current (≤ 1 year) vs non-current — the split that defines liquidity.
  • Goodwill warning: an intangible from overpaying for acquisitions; it can be written off in one stroke.
  • Deferred revenue is a good liability: cash collected before delivery — customers pre-paying you.

Cash flow

  • Three sections: operating, investing, financing.
  • FCF = OCF − capex.
  • Healthy sign: OCF ≥ net income, sustained.

Earnings-quality checks

  • NI vs OCF — a persistent gap is the classic fraud fingerprint.
  • Accruals gap = NI − OCF; big and positive = fragile earnings.
  • Receivables (and inventory) growing faster than revenue.
  • Serial "one-time" items — annual one-offs are a cost of business.
  • GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap — the wider the bridge, the harder someone is selling.

Red-flag checklist (Lesson 7)

  1. Receivables ≫ revenue growth
  2. DSO rising
  3. Inventory outpacing revenue
  4. OCF persistently below NI
  5. Serial "one-time" charges
  6. Wide GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap
  7. Governance tells: auditor changes, CFO churn, related-party deals, restatements
