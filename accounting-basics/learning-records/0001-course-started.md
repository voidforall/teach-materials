# 0001 — Course started: accounting-basics

## Context
User asked to be taught the basics of accounting, explicitly practical and investor-focused: the goal is reading company earnings and statements to make investing decisions, not bookkeeping. Full course in one session, bilingual EN/ZH.

## Decisions
- New workspace `accounting-basics/`, sibling to `valuation-basics` (which covers DCF/multiples). Accounting is positioned as the input side of valuation; lessons cross-link.
- Mission explicitly excludes journal entries/debits/credits — investor lens only.
- Source anchors: SEC investor.gov, Buffett shareholder letters, Damodaran NYU notes, Schilit's *Financial Shenanigans*, Sloan 1996 (accruals anomaly).
- Course shape: statements first (1–4), then judgment (5–7: accruals/quality, ratios/DuPont, red flags), capstone = read a real 10-K (8).

## Non-obvious lessons
- The teachable core for investors is the *articulation* of the three statements — reconciliation is the audit trail the reader gets for free.
- "Revenue is an opinion, cash is a fact" chosen as the course's through-line; recurs in lessons 4, 5, 7.
