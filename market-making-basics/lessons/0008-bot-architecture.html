Capstone: Bot Architecture

Assemble the course into a bot you can actually run: a market-data → strategy → risk → gateway pipeline, a simulator to prove it in, and a promotion ladder from backtest to paper to tiny real size.

Everything in this course maps onto one pipeline. Draw it once and keep it on the wall:

exchange ──websocket──▶ market data ──▶ strategy ──▶ risk ──▶ gateway ──▶ exchange
 (fills, acks ◀────────── account state ◀────────────────┴── (cancel/replace)
                                   │
                              logging / P&L / telemetry

The modules, each backed by a lesson

  • Market data — normalize the feed into your own book representation; compute mid, microprice, imbalance, EWMA σ (Lesson 2). Timestamp everything on arrival; a feed silently going stale must be detectable.
  • Strategy — reference price + A–S or skew quoting + toxicity ladder (Lessons 4–6). Pure function of state → desired quotes. No network calls, no persistence — so you can replay it deterministically.
  • Risk — the order gate: position, loss, kill-switch checks (Lessons 5, 7). Rejects or flattens; the strategy has no authority here.
  • Gateway — exchange API client: place/cancel, fill handling, reconnect + resubscribe logic, rate-limit handling. Own the truth about position: reconcile your books against the exchange's on every fill and periodically.
  • Telemetry — log every decision with the state that produced it. Post-fill drift stats (Lesson 6) come from here; without telemetry you cannot tell a strategy bug from a toxic regime.

The event loop, single-threaded first

A market-making bot at retail scale does not need exotic concurrency. One event loop — on book update, on fill, on timer — where each handler runs to completion, is simpler to reason about and fast enough. Make the strategy's quote update idempotent (cancel-then-replace with client order IDs), and handle the ugly realities of the gateway: lost cancels, partial fills, out-of-order messages, reconnect storms.

The promotion ladder

  1. Backtest / replay — recorded tick data, realistic fill model: your resting order fills only if price trades through your level (or you model queue position). Cheating fill models are the #1 source of fake profits.
  2. Paper trading — the real feed, real latency, simulated fills, for weeks. Measure the drift-after-fill statistic live.
  3. Tiny real size — the minimum quote size on the venue, hard limits at "losses you'd spend on a textbook". If the live P&L diverges from paper, stop and reconcile telemetry before scaling.
Keystone Build the ladder, not the plane. The deliverable of this course is not a bot that trades — it's a bot you can prove, module by module, on replayed data, with a risk layer that out-ranks the strategy. Capital is the last dependency you add, at minimum size.
Anti-pattern The optimistic fill model. Assuming your resting order fills whenever price touches your level (with no queue modeling) inflates both fill rate and spread income — the classic backtest that dies on contact with a real matching engine. Model conservatively: fill on trade-through, discount for queue depth ahead of you.

Retrieve it

Why must the strategy layer be a pure function of state?

Replayability.

No side effects means you can re-run any incident against the logged state stream and reproduce exactly what the bot did — the only way to debug live surprises.

Which module owns the "true" position, and why reconcile?

The gateway, against the exchange.

Lost fills, duplicates and reconnects make local bookkeeping drift from reality. Periodic and on-fill reconciliation bounds the drift; a position you believe wrong is un-hedgeable risk.

What makes a backtest fill model honest?

Trades through your price.

A resting order is behind the queue at its level; a mere touch doesn't fill you. Requiring trade-through (and a queue discount) makes simulated fills at least as pessimistic as reality.

Prove it to yourself

Capstone — a weekend Build the pipeline for one liquid symbol on a venue with a good testnet (e.g. a crypto exchange's testnet, or a recorded-data replay): data module → skew/A–S strategy (Lesson 5 pseudocode) → risk gate (Lesson 7 checklist) → paper gateway. Run it paper for a week. The acceptance test: (1) telemetry can answer "what was the post-fill drift, by day?"; (2) injecting a broken feed kills the bot automatically; (3) total P&L reconciles to the sum of logged fills and fees to the cent.
